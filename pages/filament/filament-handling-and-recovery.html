Filament Splicing Guide

What Filament Splicing Means

Filament splicing is joining two pieces of filament end-to-end so you can use short leftovers, continue a print without wasting a long tail, or create simple color changes without an AMS.

The goal is a straight, smooth joint that will not jam in the extruder path.

When Splicing Is Worth Doing

  • You have several short ends you would otherwise toss.
  • You are doing manual color changes without an AMS or AMS Lite.
  • You are running long prints and want to reduce spool waste.

When to Skip It

  • You rarely end up with short pieces.
  • You print very high-speed profiles where any diameter bump risks a feed issue.
  • Your filament path is extremely tight, curved, or unforgiving.

Core Principles

  • Alignment: the two ends must be straight and centered.
  • Surface area: cutting at about 45° increases bonding area.
  • Controlled heat: soften the plastic; do not boil, scorch, or drip it.
  • Diameter control: remove or trim any bulge so it feeds reliably.

DIY Splicing Methods

Method 1 — PTFE / Bowden Tube Alignment

  1. Cut both filament ends to about 45°.
  2. Slide one end into the PTFE tube.
  3. Warm both angled ends until slightly glossy or soft.
  4. Press together inside the tube and hold straight.
  5. Let cool for about 10–15 seconds.
  6. Remove and trim any bulge.

This works well because the tube forces alignment and helps control diameter.

Method 2 — Paperclip Alignment

  1. Straighten a paperclip and bend it into a shallow U channel.
  2. Cut both ends to about 45°.
  3. Heat the ends until they soften.
  4. Press together while seated in the U channel.
  5. Hold steady until cool.
  6. Lightly smooth or trim the joint.

Method 3 — Printed-Hole Alignment

Any printed part with a straight hole around 1.8–2.0 mm can act as a sleeve. Use it like the PTFE method: insert, heat, press, cool, and trim.

Method 4 — Household Sleeve

A thin rigid tube, such as a cleaned pen tube or rigid coffee stirrer, may work if it does not collapse or melt at the working distance.

Material Notes

  • PLA / PLA+: easiest to splice and cools quickly.
  • PETG: doable, but heat gently because it can string and form bigger bulges.
  • TPU: possible but tricky; go slow and keep alignment perfect.
  • ABS / Nylon: less forgiving and better with a controlled jig or tool.

Common Mistakes

  • Tape or heat-shrink usually creates a bump and can jam.
  • Super glue can become brittle and snap in the feed path.
  • Overheating causes a large bulge that will not pass cleanly.

Quick Quality Check

  1. Run your fingers along the splice; it should feel smooth.
  2. Bend gently; it should not crack immediately.
  3. If there is a bump, trim carefully and check smoothness again.

Optional Filament Splicer Tools

A filament splicer tool is a purpose-built alignment, cutting, and joining jig. It can help if you splice often, but it is not required for occasional splices.
