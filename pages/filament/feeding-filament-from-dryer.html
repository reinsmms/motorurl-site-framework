Feeding Filament Directly From a Dryer

Filament
Practical guide for printing directly from a dryer

Quick Rule

Feeding directly from a dryer keeps moisture-sensitive filament dry during the print, but the filament path must stay smooth and low-friction.

Why Feed From a Dryer?

Filament begins absorbing moisture after it leaves the dryer. For moisture-sensitive materials, this can affect print quality during longer jobs.

  • Maintains lower moisture content during the entire print.
  • Improves consistency on long jobs.
  • Reduces stringing, haze, bubbles, and mid-print degradation.

Best Uses

  • clear or translucent materials
  • PETG
  • TPU
  • Nylon, PC, or filled blends
  • large or tall prints
  • prints lasting more than about 2–3 hours

Advantages

  • Keeps filament dry throughout the print.
  • Reduces stringing and surface defects.
  • Improves clarity in transparent filaments.
  • Stabilizes extrusion on long prints.
  • Reduces repeated dry/store cycles.

Tradeoffs

  • More setup complexity.
  • Filament path and drag need attention.
  • The dryer must be positioned thoughtfully near the printer.

Example Dryer: Creality Filament Dry Box 2.0

  • No active exhaust vent.
  • Passive enclosure.
  • Single filament exit hole.
  • A slight 1–2 mm lid shim is recommended when drying PETG or similar materials so moisture vapor can escape.

Connection Options

Option 1 — Direct Feed

Best for short distances and quick testing. Place the dryer close to the printer and let the filament form a gentle arc into the printer.

Option 2 — PTFE-Guided Feed

Best for long prints or more permanent setups. Use short, gently curved 4 mm OD PTFE tubing from the dryer to the printer inlet.

Option 3 — Dryer Adapter + Push-Connect

Best for a clean, repeatable setup. Use a printed adapter and PC4-M10 push-connect fitting to guide filament from the dryer into PTFE tubing.

Drying vs Feeding Settings

  • Drying PETG before printing: about 55°C for 4–6 hours, with lid slightly shimmed.
  • Feeding or holding during printing: about 45–50°C, with the filament fed continuously to the printer.

Placement Guidelines

  • Keep the dryer on the same surface as the printer when practical.
  • Position it at or slightly below spool height.
  • Avoid sharp filament bends.
  • Avoid vertical pull directly downward.
  • Avoid side-loading the printer’s filament inlet.

Summary

Start simple with direct feed. Add PTFE guidance or fittings later if needed. The key principles are dry filament, gentle filament paths, and appropriate temperature control.
