Quick Rule

Most print problems are cause-and-effect chains: geometry, orientation, layer height, speed, cooling, and filament condition all interact.

Geometry vs Print Behavior

  • Large holes create more bridging, which can cause sagging or flattening.
  • Angled slots are often easier to print cleanly than circular holes in vertical walls.
  • Chamfers can help bridging; fillets can introduce flow variation or visible surface changes.
  • Floor-to-wall transitions can cause visible bands.

Orientation Effects

  • Aligning long edges with the X axis can produce smoother motion on some parts.
  • Poor orientation can increase travel moves and surface inconsistency.

Layer Height Effects

  • Lower layer height generally improves appearance and surface smoothness.
  • Lower layer height does not fix root causes like flow variation, geometry problems, or wet filament.
  • Smaller layers can slightly improve overhang performance.

Speed and Flow Effects

  • Outer wall speed strongly affects surface finish.
  • Printing too fast can create visible lines and inconsistent surfaces.
  • Flow variation can cause banding and subtle wall artifacts.

Cooling and Layer Time

  • Low layer time can leave layers soft, which increases sagging risk.
  • Proper cooling improves consistency and bridging.

Filament Condition

  • Wet filament can cause micro-bubbles and inconsistent extrusion.
  • Moisture often appears as faint lines, roughness, popping, stringing, or inconsistent surface texture.

Bridging and Overhangs

  • Large unsupported spans sag without support or geometry help.
  • Chamfers and tapers can improve bridging behavior.

Fillets and Geometry Complexity

  • Too many small fillets can create slicer complexity.
  • Complex geometry can cause visible layer transitions and artifacts.
